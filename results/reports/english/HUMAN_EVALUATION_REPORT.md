# Human Evaluation Poll: Modern vs. Biblical vs. Rabbinic Understandability
**Date:** [Current Date]
**Source:** `NLP-Project/results/reports/hebrew/HUMAN_EVALUATION_POLL.pdf`

## 1. Objective
To examine which historical language layer (Biblical or Rabbinic) feels closer and more understandable to Modern Hebrew speakers, and to map the main difficulties encountered in each.

## 2. General Understandability Analysis
In the study, 50 pairs of sentences were tested. Respondents were asked to indicate which sentence in each pair was more understandable to them.

**Findings:**
*   **Rabbinic Hebrew (Hazal):** Found more understandable in **51.1%** of cases.
*   **Biblical Hebrew (Mikra):** Found more understandable in **48.9%** of cases.

**Conclusion:**
There is a very slight preference for Rabbinic language, but the two languages are at a very similar level of understandability for modern speakers, with a negligible gap of about 2%.

## 3. Analysis of Difficulties
For every sentence classified as "less understandable," participants were asked to indicate the main source of difficulty.

**Distribution of Difficulties (Percentage within each language):**

| Difficulty Factor | Rabbinic Hebrew | Biblical Hebrew |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sentence Structure (Syntax)** | **41.8%** | **41.2%** |
| **Word Form (Grammar)** | 30.0% | 29.6% |
| **Vocabulary (Unfamiliar Words)** | 28.1% | 29.2% |

## 4. Conclusions
1.  **Syntax as the Main Barrier:** In both languages, the main difficulty (over 40%) is not the words themselves, but the sentence structure (e.g., VSO order/Vav-Consecutive in Bible, or complex sentences in Hazal).
2.  **Similarity in Difficulty Sources:** There is an almost identical distribution of difficulty sources between the two languages.
3.  **Overall Perception:** Modern Hebrew speakers experience Rabbinic language as slightly more accessible, but the barriers to understanding in both historical layers are primarily structural (syntax and grammar).

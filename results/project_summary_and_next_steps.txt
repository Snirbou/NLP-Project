
What We Accomplished
--------------------
We set out to determine whether **Modern Hebrew** is closer to **Biblical** or **Rabbinic** Hebrew using computational classifiers.

1.  **Data Preparation**:
    *   Processed ~730,000 sentences (Biblical, Rabbinic, Modern).
    *   Balanced the training data (undersampling Rabbinic to ~5,800 sentences to match Biblical size) to prevent bias.

2.  **Classifier Experiments**:
    *   **Manual Features Model**: Built a Random Forest using 20 engineered features (syntax, morphology, lexicon).
        *   *Result*: Modern Hebrew is **~89% Rabbinic**.
    *   **Deep Learning (DictaBERT)**: Used a state-of-the-art Hebrew LLM to generate sentence embeddings.
        *   *Result*: Modern Hebrew is **95-99% Rabbinic**.

3.  **Key Insight (The "Paradox")**:
    *   Our **statistical averages** (Layer 2) suggested Modern was closer to Biblical.
    *   Our **classifiers** (Layer 4) proved it is overwhelmingly Rabbinic.
    *   *Resolution*: Modern Hebrew uses **Biblical Vocabulary** (tricking simple stats) but strictly follows **Rabbinic Grammar/Structure** (which classifiers detect).

Main Results
------------
*   **Structure Wins**: Despite using Biblical words, the grammatical skeleton of Modern Hebrew (Tense system, SVO word order, 'Shel' possession) is undeniably Rabbinic.
*   **Genre Nuance**: Formal registers (News, Medical) feel slightly more "Biblical" (5-20%) than informal ones (Blogs, Forums), but even they remain structurally Rabbinic.
